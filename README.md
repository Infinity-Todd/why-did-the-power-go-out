# Why Did the Power Go Out?

**Chenyi Zhu · UC San Diego**

Does the mix of reported causes change between summer and winter? We compared 513 summer outages and 369 winter outages in the continental United States from 2000 through 2015. Severe weather accounted for about half of the reports in both seasons. The largest gap was for intentional attacks, which accounted for 17.0% of summer reports and 28.2% of winter reports.

Across all seven reported causes, the total variation distance between the seasons was 0.121. Only 20 of 10,000 random shuffles of the season labels produced a difference at least that large. This suggests that the mix of reported causes differed between summer and winter.

[Source data from Purdue University](https://engineering.purdue.edu/LASCI/research-data/outages/outage.xlsx)
